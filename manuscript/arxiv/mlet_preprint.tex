\begin{quote}
\small
Machine Learning Evapotranspiration (MLET) separates two targets. Reference
evapotranspiration (ETo, denoted $\eto$ below) describes atmospheric demand for
a defined reference surface. Actual evapotranspiration ($\etaactual$) also
depends on crop condition, soil water, and management. MLET therefore uses two
evidence paths: retrospective OpenET actual-ET prediction and a prospective
20-day reference-ETo artifact for Idaho. The reproduced Phase~2 record contains
\PhaseTwoN{}
station-days from \PhaseTwoStations{} stations. The preregistered comparison
is M3 OpenETRidge against B2 WeatherRidge. M3 has mean absolute error (MAE)
\MThreeMAE{} $\mmday$ versus \BTwoMAE{} $\mmday$ for B2. This is a
\HtwoDelta{} $\mmday$ improvement and a \HtwoReduction\% reduction. A
\BootstrapPhaseTwo-replicate station-blocked bootstrap gives a 95\% interval
of [\HtwoCILow{}, \HtwoCIHigh{}] $\mmday$. M2 OpenETRecal has the lowest MAE
among B1, B2, and M1 through M3 on \PhaseTwoN{} common fitted-model rows at
\MTwoMAE{} $\mmday$. It is not the preregistered arm. The
prospective path applies the American Society of Civil Engineers Environmental
and Water Resources Institute (ASCE-EWRI) standardized short-reference equation
to archived ensemble members \citep{asce2005}. One real issue, one station, and
\FeasibilityN{} targets pass source and identity checks. That retrospective
reforecast diagnostic has MAE \FeasibilityMAE{} $\mmday$, while prior-station
climatology has MAE \FeasibilityBaselineMAE{} $\mmday$. The signed
baseline-minus-forecast MAE difference is \FeasibilityImprovement{} $\mmday$, so
forecast MAE is higher. Empirical p10-to-p90 coverage is
\FeasibilityCoverage{} against a nominal coverage target of
\FeasibilityNominalCoverage{}, and mean band width is \FeasibilityWidth{}
$\mmday$. One bootstrap cluster and fewer than \SupportMinimum{} targets per
support cell prevent a confidence interval and a skill claim.
\end{quote}

\section{Introduction}

Evapotranspiration connects the land surface, the atmosphere, and water
management. Reference $\eto$ measures atmospheric demand over a defined
reference surface \citep{allen1998,asce2005}. Actual $\etaactual$ is the water
flux from the observed crop and soil system. It responds to water stress, crop
development, soil condition, and management. A retrospective actual-ET
estimate and a prospective reference-$\eto$ outlook can therefore be
individually valid and jointly misleading when they share one label.

Satellite ET products provide broad retrospective information. OpenET joins
several remote-sensing models and has been evaluated against in situ ET
observations across the contiguous United States \citep{volk2024}. Weather
ensembles provide a different evidence source. They can drive a standardized
reference-$\eto$ equation for future dates, but they do not observe future
crop or soil state. The scientific question is not only whether a model has a
low error. The question is also whether the target, issue time, baseline, and
claim remain unchanged from data acquisition to publication.

This paper reports one completed comparison and one incomplete outlook
evaluation. The completed comparison asks whether an OpenET feature improves
retrospective daily actual-ET prediction under station-held-out 10-fold
evaluation. The outlook protocol asks whether archived Global Ensemble
Forecast System version 12 (GEFSv12) weather can support a 20-day
reference-$\eto$ artifact. The source is provided by the National Oceanic and
Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). The artifact uses an independent
station-derived target. The protocol fails closed when source time, station
identity, or evaluation support is incomplete. A frozen protocol is fixed
before evaluation. Promotion means public operational release. Validation
complete means that every required support cell meets its target count. A
skillful result has the preregistered MAE reduction and a confidence interval
above zero. Release review is an independent check before any public release.

The work makes four contributions. First, it defines a target ontology that
prevents actual ET, reference $\eto$, and conditional crop ET from becoming
interchangeable. Second, it reproduces a checksum-bound Phase~2 comparison and
reports its preregistered model arm. Third, it implements a source-issue-aligned
retrospective reforecast
reference-$\eto$ artifact with quantiles, source receipts, and station-to-grid
identity. Fourth, it exposes an incomplete evaluation as a machine-readable
state. This last contribution prevents one feasible case from becoming a
validation claim.

Each public claim must bind six identities: the physical target, the source
artifact, the forecast issue, the valid date, the independent target, and the
evaluation split. A missing identity is not imputed. The evaluator returns an
incomplete state. The state can contain diagnostic metrics, but it cannot
contain a promotion decision.

\section{Data and evidence artifacts}

\subsection{Retrospective actual-ET data}

The Phase~2 build joins daily OpenET model ET with a public flux-tower ET
archive on exact station and date keys. The source manifest binds the two
archives by identifiers and checksums. The joined dataset has \JoinedRows{}
rows from \JoinedStations{} stations. It has \JoinedLabels{} non-gap labels.
The response $y_i$ is the energy-balance-corrected daily actual ET in
$\mmday$. The uncorrected \code{ET} column is never scored. The benchmark
reference ETo value $E_i^g$ is gridMET ETo. It is not GEFS ETo. The OpenET
input $x_i^{\mathrm{OpenET}}$ is the available daily OpenET actual-ET estimate
in $\mmday$.

The ridge-model comparison uses the common predictor-ready table. The runner
drops rows with a missing measured response, OpenET value, gridMET ETo value, or
weather covariate. It also drops nonfinite weather covariates. It retains finite
signed response values. This table has \PhaseTwoN{} station-days from
\PhaseTwoStations{} stations. B1 fits only rows with $E_i^g>0$.

The OpenET source data and the flux benchmark follow the Phase~II accuracy
study \citep{volk2024}. The present experiment does not re-estimate that
study's site-level accuracy. It asks a narrower prediction question under one
repository-frozen split.

\subsection{Prospective weather data}

The outlook uses the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA)
Global Ensemble Forecast System version 12 (GEFSv12) weekly reforecast archive
\citep{ncep2021}. Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) is the time basis for source
receipts. A 00Z (00:00 UTC) issue starts at midnight UTC. The frozen schedule
contains the Wednesday 00Z issues from 2013-01-02 through 2019-12-25. This is
365 planned issues. The feasibility receipt covers one issue, 2019-07-03 00Z.
It finds \GEFSObjects{} of
\GEFSObjects{} planned objects and \GEFSBytes{} advertised bytes. These are
exact inventory values for one issue. They are not a transfer-performance
benchmark.

The decoder produces \GEFSRows{} weather rows from \GEFSGrids{} points in the
common 0.5-degree GEFS grid-point subset, \GEFSMembers{} ensemble members, and
\GEFSValidDates{} valid dates. This subset is the exact intersection of the
0.25-degree early source segment and the 0.5-degree late source segment. No
interpolation creates it. The identity
$\GEFSGrids\times\GEFSMembers\times\GEFSValidDates=\GEFSRows$ is checked before
the $\eto$ builder runs. Each normalized artifact contains its raw-object
receipt and SHA-256 (a 256-bit secure hash) digest.

\subsection{Independent AgriMet targets}

The U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (USBR) AgriMet network publishes daily
grass-reference evapotranspiration (ETos) as reference evapotranspiration
(ETo). The American Society of Civil Engineers Environmental and Water
Resources Institute (ASCE-EWRI) standard defines this grass reference
\citep{asce2005}. The network and station records are documented by USBR
\citep{usbrarchive,usbrlocations}. ETos is a published station-derived target.
The recorded AgriMet retrieval time proves later access to the target. It does
not prove its original publication time. It is not a direct flux measurement
and it is not a GEFS recomputation. The
acquisition adapter parses the official data boundary. It preserves missing
markers as exclusions. It converts each non-missing value from inches per day
with the exact factor 25.4.

The current station snapshot has \AgriMetCurrentStations{} stations, including
\AgriMetIdahoStations{} in Idaho. This snapshot does not establish historical
coordinates. Historical page evidence supports \AgriMetHistoricalStations{}
stations. Their acquisition record contains \AgriMetRows{} daily values and
\AgriMetExclusions{} exclusions from 2015-06-17 through 2019-12-31. The
maximum observed station-to-grid distance is 25.475 km across these 19
mappings. The frozen limit is 50 km. This mapping is station identity, not
field geometry.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Phase 2 station-held-out evaluation}

The primary station-held-out 10-fold evaluation assigns stations to
$k=10$ deterministic folds with seed \PhaseTwoSeed. A fitted model never sees
a row from its held-out station. Feature means, feature scales, coefficients,
and the crop coefficient use training rows only. A separate split trains
before 2019-01-01 and describes later rows. That time split is not the primary
decision split.

The experiment fixes six models. B0 persistence uses the previous labeled day
at one station inside the predictor-ready common table used by the runner. Its
\PersistenceN{} consecutive-day pairs make it an oracle-like diagnostic. B0 is
separate from the common-sample comparison and from H2. B1 uses one static
pooled training-set mean of $y_i/E_i^g$ over rows
with $E_i^g>0$:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \widehat K_c&=
  \frac{1}{|\mathcal T_+|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal T_+}
  \frac{y_i}{E_i^g},\\
  \mathcal T_+&=\{i\in\mathcal T:E_i^g>0\},
  \qquad \widehat y_i=\widehat K_c E_i^g.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:kc}
\end{equation}

B1 is not crop-specific or stage-specific. B2 is ridge regression on gridMET
ETo, mean temperature, vapor-pressure deficit, wind speed, and sine and
cosine day-of-year terms. M1 uses $x_i^{\mathrm{OpenET}}$ directly. M2 is
ordinary least squares with an intercept and slope:

\begin{equation}
  \widehat y_i=\widehat\alpha+\widehat\beta x_i^{\mathrm{OpenET}}.
  \label{eq:m2}
\end{equation}

M3 adds OpenET to all B2 predictors. B2 and M3 standardize every predictor
with its training mean and standard deviation, then solve

\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}=
  \arg\min_{\boldsymbol\beta}
  \left\{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}
  (y_i-\mathbf x_i^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\beta)^2
  +\lambda\lVert\boldsymbol\beta\rVert_2^2\right\},
  \qquad \lambda=1.
  \label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}

The ridge intercept is the training response mean and is not penalized.

\subsection{Deterministic metrics and H2}

For prediction $\widehat y_i$ and observed target $y_i$, define the residual
$e_{m,i}=\widehat y_{m,i}-y_i$ for model $m$. The experiment reports mean
absolute error (MAE), root mean square error (RMSE), and signed bias:

\begin{align}
  \operatorname{MAE} &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|e_{m,i}|,
  \label{eq:mae}\\
  \operatorname{RMSE} &=
  \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{m,i}^2},
  \label{eq:rmse}\\
  \operatorname{Bias} &=
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{m,i}.
  \label{eq:bias}
\end{align}

H2 (the preregistered OpenET comparison) is a comparison, not a model. It
requires at least 10\% lower pooled MAE for M3 than the better of B1 and B2,
with a station-blocked 95\% confidence interval for the baseline-minus-M3 MAE
difference above zero. B2 wins the OpenET-free selection. M2 has the lowest
MAE among B1, B2, and M1 through M3 on $\PhaseTwoN{}$ common fitted-model
rows. B0 remains a separate oracle-like diagnostic on $n=\PersistenceN{}$
consecutive pairs. The paired station-day contrast is

\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{MAE}}=
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}
  \left(|e_{B2,i}|-|e_{M3,i}|\right),
  \qquad r=\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{MAE}}}{\operatorname{MAE}_{B2}}.
  \label{eq:h2}
\end{equation}

Inference resamples stations, not rows. It uses \BootstrapPhaseTwo{}
replicates and seed \PhaseTwoSeed. The reported interval is the percentile
interval of the paired baseline-minus-M3 MAE difference \citep{efron1993}.

\subsection{Weather conversion and reference ETo}

The GEFS decoder converts each member to local daily ASCE inputs. Air
temperature uses $T_C=T_K-273.15$. Stored GEFS wind is measured at 10 m.
The decoder forms the vector magnitude
$u_{10}=\sqrt{u^2+v^2}$, and \code{pyfao56} performs the internal 10 m to 2 m
adjustment. Vapor pressure follows

\begin{equation}
  e_a=\frac{q p}{\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)q}\frac{1}{1000},
  \qquad \epsilon=0.622,
  \label{eq:vapor}
\end{equation}

where $q$ is specific humidity in $\mathrm{kg\,kg^{-1}}$, $p$ is pressure in
Pa, and $e_a$ is actual vapor pressure in kPa. Radiation changes from
$\mathrm{J\,m^{-2}}$ to $\mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}}$. Interval midpoints assign
weather intervals to the America/Boise civil day. A UTC-day aggregate fails
schema validation.

For each member, the code calls the ASCE short-reference equation
\citep{asce2005,thorp2022}:

\begin{equation}
  \eto=
  \frac{0.408\Delta(R_n-G)+
  \gamma\frac{900}{T+273}u_2(e_s-e_a)}
  {\Delta+\gamma(1+0.34u_2)}.
  \label{eq:asce}
\end{equation}

Here $\eto$ has units $\mmday$. The symbols are defined as follows. $T$ is
mean air temperature ($\mathrm{degrees\,C}$). $R_n$ is net radiation and $G$
is soil heat flux, both in $\mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}\,day^{-1}}$. $\Delta$ is the
vapor-pressure slope and $\gamma$ is the psychrometric constant, both in
$\mathrm{kPa\,degree\,C^{-1}}$. $u_2$ is 2 m wind speed in
$\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. $e_s$ is saturation vapor pressure and $e_a$ is actual
vapor pressure, both in kPa. The implementation calls the repository's
vendored pyfao56 ASCE daily routine. The implementation map binds version 1.4.3
to the vendored version file.

Let $E_{g\ell j}$ be Eq.~\ref{eq:asce} for grid point $g$, lead $\ell$, and
member $j$. The artifact stores

\begin{equation}
  q_{g\ell}(\alpha)=Q_{\alpha}
  \left(\{E_{g\ell j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\right),
  \qquad \alpha\in\{0.10,0.50,0.90\}.
  \label{eq:quantiles}
\end{equation}

The feasibility case has $m=\GEFSMembers$. For each lead, the $m$ values are
sorted. The empirical p10, p50, and p90 use NumPy linear interpolation between
adjacent order statistics at probabilities 0.10, 0.50, and 0.90. The p10 to
p90 result is an uncalibrated ensemble quantile band. The contract rejects
duplicate members, missing leads, negative values, and unordered quantiles.

\subsection{Issue time and independent target}

For issue $t_0$ and lead $\ell$, the valid date is

\begin{equation}
  d(t_0,\ell)=
  \operatorname{date}_{\mathrm{America/Boise}}(t_0)+\ell,
  \qquad \ell\in\{1,\ldots,20\}.
  \label{eq:date}
\end{equation}

The recorded source issue time identifies $t_0$. A later archive retrieval
timestamp records later access. It does not prove operational availability at
$t_0$. The AgriMet target's later retrieval timestamp likewise does not prove
its original publication time. The target must become available only after its
completed local day. These later retrieval dates do not expand either
information boundary.

For station $s$ and date $d$, the independent target is

\begin{equation}
  y_{sd}=25.4\,\operatorname{ETos}^{\mathrm{in/day}}_{sd}.
  \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}

The code does not recompute this target from GEFS. It does not replace a
missing ETos value with zero or interpolation.

\subsection{Leakage-safe climatology}

The fixed climatology uses all strictly prior calendar years for the same
station and target day of year. It is not a learned cross-fold component. For
target valid date $d$, let $Y_d=\operatorname{year}(d)$ and
$k=\operatorname{doy}(d)$. The target date, not the issue date, sets $Y_d$.
For each station and target date,

\begin{equation}
  b_{s,d}=
  \frac{1}{|\mathcal Y_{s,k,Y_d}|}
  \sum_{Y\in\mathcal Y_{s,k,Y_d}} y_{s,k,Y},
  \quad
  \mathcal Y_{s,k,Y_d}=\{Y:Y<Y_d\}.
  \label{eq:climatology}
\end{equation}

The evaluated target year and all future years are absent. A lead that crosses
New Year uses the year of its target valid date. A missing prior-year set
creates an explicit exclusion.

\subsection{Probabilistic metrics and support}

The outcome protocol reports p50 MAE, RMSE, and bias. It also reports empirical
p10-to-p90 coverage and mean band width:

\begin{align}
  C_{10,90}&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}
  \mathbf 1\{q_{0.10,i}\le y_i\le q_{0.90,i}\},
  \label{eq:coverage}\\
  W_{10,90}&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}
  (q_{0.90,i}-q_{0.10,i}).
  \label{eq:width}
\end{align}

\begin{figure*}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.97\textwidth]{figure_2_phase2_models.pdf}
\caption{Station-held-out actual-ET MAE. B2 and M3 form the preregistered H2
  comparison. Their common sample contains $n=\PhaseTwoN{}$ rows from
  \PhaseTwoStations{} stations. M2 has the lowest MAE among B1, B2, and M1
  through M3 on $\PhaseTwoN{}$ common fitted-model rows. It does not inherit
  the M3 interval. B0 uses $n=\PersistenceN{}$ consecutive-day pairs inside the
  predictor-ready common table. It is excluded from H2.}
  \label{fig:phase2-models}
\end{figure*}

For quantile level $\alpha$, pinball loss is

\begin{equation}
  \rho_{\alpha}(y,q)=
  \begin{cases}
    \alpha(y-q), & y\ge q,\\
    (\alpha-1)(y-q), & y<q.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:pinball}
\end{equation}

The mean score averages Eq.~\ref{eq:pinball} over observations and the three
stored quantiles \citep{koenker1978,gneiting2007}. It is not the continuous
ranked probability score.

Spatial folds use the target grid cell. Grid 43.50:-116.00 maps to the
one-degree tile 43:-116. The fold is the integer value of the full SHA-256
hexadecimal digest for that tile modulo 5. The BOII target maps to fold 2.

The evaluator crosses 20 leads and four seasons: December-January-February
(DJF), March-April-May (MAM), June-July-August (JJA), and
September-October-November (SON). Five folds give \SupportCells{} cells. Each
cell needs at least \SupportMinimum{} paired targets, so the minimum support
count is \SupportMinimumTotal{} cell observations. The paired ETo interval uses
\BootstrapETo{} cluster-bootstrap replicates with seed \EToSeed. A cluster is
one local issue date and target station. An interval requires at least two
clusters.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What the Phase 2 result establishes}

The completed result supports one narrow conclusion. Under the frozen
station-held-out split, M3 OpenETRidge reduces retrospective daily actual-ET MAE
against B2 WeatherRidge. The paired interval accounts for station clustering.
It does not account for a new geographic domain, a new label system, or a
future weather issue. The comparison therefore supports OpenET value within
this benchmark. It does not validate the outlook.

M2 creates an important reporting test. Its point MAE is lower than M3. A
reader could reasonably call M2 the descriptive winner. The preregistered H2
arm is still M3 because the runner fixed that model. A paper must report both
facts. Combining M2's point error with M3's interval would create a result that
no execution produced.

The persistence row has a much lower MAE than every fitted model. That value
uses only \PersistenceN{} consecutive-day pairs and consumes the previous
observed label. It is not available for a lead-1 forecast without an observed
prior day. We therefore treat it as an oracle-like diagnostic, not the H2
baseline.

\subsection{What the feasibility case establishes}

The reference-$\eto$ case shows that the data path can produce an auditable
artifact. The path starts with an archived issue and ends with a later,
independent station target. It preserves local-day semantics, members,
quantiles, source receipts, and checksums. This is a systems result.

The diagnostic forecast is worse than the simple climatology for the one case.
Its median forecast also has a positive bias of \FeasibilityBias{}
$\mmday$. The empirical p10-p90 band covers 25\% of the 20 targets. These
numbers warn against promotion. They do not identify a stable lead effect,
season effect, or statewide effect. One issue and one station cannot estimate
those effects.

The incomplete state is scientifically useful. It prevents source feasibility
from becoming forecast validation. It also preserves the negative diagnostic
instead of deleting it. A full result must include every frozen support cell,
even when the candidate does not improve on climatology.

\subsection{Why target separation matters}

Reference $\eto$ is a standardized reference evapotranspiration rate. Actual ET is a realized
surface flux. An actual-ET outlook also needs crop state, root-zone water, and
management assumptions. A crop coefficient can convert $\eto$ to potential
crop ET under named assumptions. It cannot by itself create unconditional
actual ET.

This boundary affects model assessment. A retrospective satellite feature can
help predict historical actual ET. That evidence does not make the feature
available at a future issue. An AgriMet ETos target can assess future reference
$\eto$. That evidence does not assess field-scale actual ET. The ontology in
Table~\ref{tab:ontology} keeps these claims separate by construction.

\section{Limitations}

The paper has six main limitations.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The full 365-issue outcome archive is absent. Reference-$\eto$ skill
  remains \claimpending.

  \item The current AgriMet registry is not a historical movement ledger.
  Historical page evidence covers \AgriMetHistoricalStations{} stations.

  \item The BOII location evidence brackets the case date. It does not prove
  an unchanged coordinate for every date from 2013 through 2019.

  \item The spatial artifact contains weather-grid points. It does not contain
  field boundaries, crop labels, or area weights.

  \item The Phase~2 comparison uses public flux labels and historical OpenET.
  It does not measure future forecast performance.

  \item The one-case ETo metrics have $n=\FeasibilityN{}$ and one cluster.
  Their uncertainty is not estimable under the frozen bootstrap.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}

\mlet{} separates retrospective actual ET from prospective reference $\eto$.
The Phase~2 record supports a preregistered M3 versus B2 actual-ET result. M3
reduces MAE by \HtwoReduction\%, with a paired 95\% interval of
[\HtwoCILow{}, \HtwoCIHigh{}] $\mmday$. M2 has lower point MAE than B1, B2,
M1, and M3 on $\PhaseTwoN{}$ common fitted-model rows, but it has no recorded
paired interval.

The reference-$\eto$ path produces a real, checksum-bound candidate and an
independent target. Its one-case diagnostic is worse than climatology and has
insufficient support. The paper therefore makes no reference-$\eto$ skill or
irrigation claim. Promotion requires the complete frozen hindcast and a
separate release review.
