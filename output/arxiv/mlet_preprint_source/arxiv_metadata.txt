Title: MLET: Selective Neural Residual Correction for Spatial Evapotranspiration
Author: Marco Trotta

Abstract:
A neural correction of a satellite estimate must decide both what to predict and when its correction is useful. We study this decision as selective residual regression under spatial transfer and input corruption. Using 7,923 OpenET station-days from 85 stations, we compare ensemble disagreement, input-support distance, and learned relative benefit. Selectors use spatially cross-fitted neural predictions; a separate evaluation withholds both locations and later years. The experiment retains a fixed satellite fallback and scores every test observation. A natural humidity anomaly motivates a separate feature-omission arm and controlled test-input faults. After VPD omission, a tenfold wind-input fault raises residual-network station MAE from 0.829 to 1.415 mm/day on 7,873 matched observations. Support screening yields 0.857, but largely through fallback: it accepts only 1.74% of the station-weighted population. At matched 50% acceptance, its advantage over disagreement is inconclusive. However, the learned benefit ranking is worse than the support ranking by 0.243 mm/day, with paired interval [0.110, 0.412], under that same fault. The later-year evaluation does not confirm this ordering, and natural-test gains do not transfer uniformly to croplands. We derive the baseline-relative risk objective, preserve all negative controls, and distinguish screening from ranking improvements. The result isolates a failure of cross-fitted benefit selection under a specified input fault and separates correction ranking from fallback frequency.
